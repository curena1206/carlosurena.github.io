Payments Franchise Index · PFI

See the payments franchise beyond volume and revenue.

The Payments Franchise Index is a structured diagnostic for evaluating how effectively a payments business converts its capabilities, activity, and client relationships into sustainable commercial value.

It makes the underlying economics more visible by examining how the franchise earns, serves clients, operates, and manages the portfolio. Pricing, monetization, infrastructure, and management discipline are evaluated as connected conditions rather than isolated measures.

42 operator questions · Approximately 3–5 minutes · No data stored · PDF report available

Run the Payments Franchise Index↗

The Payments Franchise Index began with a conclusion that changed when the frame widened.

I evaluated a product that appeared to have limited value on its own. When I considered its role in the broader franchise, the commercial conclusion changed.

The product-level analysis was not wrong. It was incomplete. The more important question was what role the product played in the broader franchise beyond its isolated performance.

The PFI grew from that question. It helps leaders evaluate the connections among products, capabilities, economics, and dependencies. It also tests whether management disciplines support the franchise as a whole.

It does not replace financial analysis, product expertise, or management judgment. It identifies where the portfolio requires a wider view.

Six dimensions of franchise strength

01

Revenue architecture

How the franchise monetizes payment activity and how clearly revenue sources, concentration, and economic dependencies are understood.

02

Pricing discipline

How consistently pricing reflects client value, flow characteristics, market context, and portfolio economics.

03

Client value

How payment capabilities support client needs, relationship value, adoption, and durable commercial relevance.

04

Operating maturity

How effectively execution capacity, servicing requirements, exception activity, and governance support the portfolio.

05

Infrastructure and dependencies

How rails, routing, network relationships, technology, liquidity, and external dependencies influence economic and strategic flexibility.

06

Portfolio management

How consistently leaders evaluate performance, trade-offs, investment priorities, structural risks, and growth opportunities across the franchise.

About This Work

Developed from the commercial questions that have defined my career

I am a commercial strategy executive whose career has been built primarily in payments.

I developed the Payments Franchise Index to make the underlying economics of a payments business more visible. It improves how leaders evaluate structural weakness and dependency risk while identifying monetization opportunities and portfolio priorities.

It reflects a lesson learned through experience: the commercial meaning of a product can change when it is evaluated as part of the franchise rather than on its own.

Explore my commercial perspective→

The Payments Franchise Index is a directional management diagnostic. Its output depends on the completeness and accuracy of the information provided and should be interpreted alongside financial, operational, product, client, risk, and regulatory evidence.

The PFI does not constitute financial, legal, regulatory, investment, or professional advice. It does not evaluate any organization without information supplied by the user, and responses are not stored beyond the browser session unless the user chooses to save the generated report.

The score is a starting point. The value is in the questions it makes visible.

If the results surface an important commercial or portfolio question, I welcome a conversation about what the findings may mean in context.
