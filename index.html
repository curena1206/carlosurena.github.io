Perspective

Building payment products requires more than building capabilities.

Successful payment products must solve real client problems, operate reliably and create sustainable value. Doing that requires understanding how product decisions affect clients, technology, operations, risk and the economics of the business.

Three principles shape how I approach payments product management.

Start with the client problem.

Before developing a new capability or changing an existing product, understand the problem it needs to solve. Technology creates possibilities, but client needs, adoption and practical application determine whether those possibilities translate into value.

Connect product decisions across the business.

A product decision rarely affects just one function. Product, Technology, Operations, Risk and Finance bring different perspectives. Understanding those dependencies early helps turn product strategy into something the organization can actually deliver and support.

Understand the economics behind the product.

Revenue and transaction growth matter, but they don't tell the entire story. Understanding profitability, client behavior and the economics of delivering a service helps determine where to invest, what to improve and which capabilities are worth preserving.

Original Work

Payments Franchise Index

Over two decades in payments, I repeatedly encountered the same challenge: organizations can see individual performance metrics while still struggling to understand where friction is being created across the broader payments business.

I developed the Payments Franchise Index to explore that problem systematically.

PFI examines how effectively an organization can see and connect signals across client behavior, growth, revenue, execution, economics and governance. Its purpose is not to prescribe a strategy or replace deeper analysis. It is to identify where to look.

PFI identifies where to look; deeper examination determines what, if anything, should change.
