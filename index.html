Perspective

Commercial performance is often easier to measure than to understand.

Revenue, volume, adoption and execution tell part of the story. But the decisions that determine long-term value often sit underneath those visible outcomes - in the economics of the portfolio, the dependencies between functions, and the way capabilities translate into client value.

That leads to three convictions that shape how I approach commercial decisions.

Look beneath the visible outcome.

Growth, revenue or activity can appear healthy while the underlying economics tell a different story. Better decisions begin by understanding what is actually creating value, where value is being lost, and which trade-offs the headline measures do not reveal.

Connect the decision across the enterprise.

The most consequential commercial decisions rarely belong to one function. Product, Finance, Sales, Operations, Risk and Technology each see part of the answer. Stronger decisions come from connecting those perspectives and understanding how choices in one part of the business affect the economics of another.

Design capability, economics and client value together.

Building a capability is not the same as creating value. Sustainable performance comes when what the organization can deliver, what clients genuinely need and will adopt, and how the business creates economic value are considered together.

A decision in practice

Sometimes the right decision becomes clearer when you understand why clients still need the product.

In one portfolio review, a low-margin payment capability was being considered for discontinuation. Looking more closely at client usage showed that it supported specific trade-finance needs for which other payment methods were not complete substitutes.

The decision shifted from whether to eliminate the product to how to preserve the client value while changing its access and economics. With competitors exiting the business, retaining the capability also preserved a point of differentiation. The capability remained in the portfolio, client usage held, and the economics improved.

I built that discipline more than once, independently, at three separate institutions across my career. The Payments Franchise Index is the systematized version of what I kept rebuilding by hand.
